Top CV Mistakes That Could Cost You the Job

5 min read

A well-written CV can open the door to interviews, while a poorly prepared one may be rejected within seconds. Recruiters often review dozens or even hundreds of applications for a single position, which means small mistakes can have a significant impact. Understanding the most common CV mistakes can help you present yourself more professionally and improve your chances of getting noticed.

1. Using a Generic CV for Every Job

One of the biggest mistakes is sending the same CV to every employer. Different positions require different skills and experience. Tailoring your CV to match the job description shows that you understand the role and have taken the time to prepare your application.

2. Including Irrelevant Information

Recruiters want to quickly understand why you are a good fit for the position. Avoid filling your CV with outdated jobs, unrelated hobbies or unnecessary personal details that do not support your application.

3. Focusing on Responsibilities Instead of Achievements

Listing daily tasks tells employers what your job involved, but achievements demonstrate the value you created. Whenever possible, include measurable results that show your impact.

Instead of: Managed customer support.

Try: Improved customer satisfaction by resolving over 95% of support requests within 24 hours.

4. Poor Formatting

A cluttered layout can make even strong experience difficult to read. Use consistent fonts, clear headings and enough white space to create a professional appearance. Your CV should guide the reader naturally from one section to the next.

5. Spelling and Grammar Errors

Simple mistakes can create the impression that you rushed your application or overlooked important details. Always proofread your CV carefully and consider asking someone else to review it before sending it.

6. Making Your CV Too Long

A CV does not need to include every job you have ever had. Focus on the most relevant experience and keep the content concise. For many applicants, one or two pages are enough to communicate their qualifications effectively.

7. Ignoring Keywords

Many employers use applicant tracking systems (ATS) to filter applications before a recruiter even sees them. Including relevant keywords from the job description can improve your chances of passing these automated screenings.

8. Forgetting to Update Your Contact Information

It sounds obvious, but outdated phone numbers, inactive email addresses or broken portfolio links still appear on many CVs. Double-check that employers can easily contact you.

A successful CV is clear, relevant and focused on demonstrating your value. Avoiding these common mistakes will not guarantee a job offer, but it will greatly improve your chances of making a positive first impression. Take the time to review your CV carefully before every application, and remember that small improvements can make a significant difference over time.
