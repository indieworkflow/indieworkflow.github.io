How to Write a Cover Letter That Gets Noticed

8 min read

A cover letter gives you an opportunity to explain why you are interested in a position and why you could be a strong fit for the role. While your CV focuses mainly on your experience, skills and achievements, a cover letter allows you to connect those qualifications to the specific job you are applying for.

A strong cover letter does not need to be long. It needs to be relevant, clear and written specifically for the position. The goal is to make the employer want to learn more about you rather than simply repeat everything already listed on your CV.

Do You Really Need a Cover Letter?

Not every job application requires a cover letter. Some employers make it optional, while others specifically request one. Even when it is optional, submitting a thoughtful cover letter can give you another opportunity to demonstrate your interest and explain why you are a good match.

However, sending the same generic letter with every application can do more harm than good. If you decide to include one, make sure it adds useful information instead of simply repeating your CV.

1. Start With a Strong Opening

The first paragraph should quickly explain which position you are applying for and why you are interested in it. Avoid starting with a generic sentence that could apply to any company or job.

Instead of writing:

Weak: I am writing to apply for the position advertised on your website.

Try to make the opening more specific:

Better: I was excited to see your opening for a Customer Support Specialist because my experience helping customers and solving problems closely matches the requirements of the role.

A specific opening immediately gives the employer context and makes the letter feel more personal.

2. Explain Why You Are Interested in the Role

Employers want to know that you have a genuine reason for applying. Your cover letter should demonstrate that you understand what the company or position involves.

You do not need to write a long paragraph praising the company. Instead, mention something specific about the role, product, industry or responsibilities that interests you.

For example, if you are applying for a marketing position, you could explain that you are interested in helping the company grow its online presence or work with a particular type of audience.

3. Connect Your Skills to the Job

One of the most important parts of a cover letter is showing how your experience can help the employer.

Do not simply list skills such as communication, teamwork or problem-solving. Explain how you have used those skills and why they are relevant to the position.

For example:

Weak: I have excellent communication and organizational skills.

Better: In my previous role, I regularly handled multiple customer requests while keeping accurate records and following up on unresolved issues.

The second example provides evidence instead of simply making a claim.

4. Focus on Achievements

Your CV should already contain your main achievements, but your cover letter can highlight one or two that are particularly relevant to the position.

Whenever possible, use specific results. Numbers, improvements and measurable outcomes can make your experience more convincing.

For example, instead of saying that you managed social media accounts, you could explain that you helped increase engagement, grow an audience or improve the performance of specific campaigns.

The key is relevance. You do not need to mention every achievement you have ever had. Choose the examples that best demonstrate why you could succeed in the role.

5. Show What You Can Bring to the Company

A common mistake is making the entire cover letter about what you want from the employer.

It is natural to mention your career goals and interest in the position, but employers also want to understand what you can contribute.

Think about the problems the company is trying to solve and explain how your skills, experience or approach could help.

This changes the cover letter from a simple request for employment into a short explanation of the value you could provide.

6. Keep It Short and Easy to Read

A cover letter should not become a second CV. In most situations, one page is enough.

Use short paragraphs and remove information that does not directly support your application. A hiring manager should be able to quickly understand who you are, why you are interested and what makes you relevant.

Avoid complicated language simply to sound professional. Clear and natural writing is usually more effective than long sentences filled with unnecessary words.

7. Avoid Generic Templates

Templates can help you structure a cover letter, but copying a generic letter and changing only the company name is easy to recognize.

A better approach is to use a basic structure and customize the important sections for every application.

Before sending your letter, check:

  • Does it mention the correct company?
  • Does it mention the correct position?
  • Are the examples relevant to the job?
  • Does it explain why you are interested?
  • Does it show what you can contribute?

8. Avoid Common Cover Letter Mistakes

Even a strong application can be weakened by small mistakes. Always proofread your cover letter before sending it.

  • Using the wrong company or hiring manager name
  • Repeating the entire CV
  • Writing several pages of unnecessary information
  • Using overly formal or unnatural language
  • Making unsupported claims about your abilities
  • Failing to explain why you want the specific role
  • Sending the same letter to every employer

Take a few minutes to read the letter from the employer's perspective. If you removed the company name, could the same letter be sent to ten other companies? If the answer is yes, it probably needs more personalization.

A Simple Cover Letter Structure

If you are unsure where to begin, use this simple structure:

  • Paragraph 1: Introduce yourself and state the position you are applying for.
  • Paragraph 2: Explain why you are interested in the role or company.
  • Paragraph 3: Highlight your most relevant skills, experience or achievements.
  • Paragraph 4: Explain the value you could bring and finish with a clear, professional closing.

This structure gives you enough space to make a strong case without turning the cover letter into a long essay.

Before You Send Your Cover Letter

Before submitting your application, take a final moment to review the entire letter.

  • Check spelling and grammar.
  • Make sure the company and position names are correct.
  • Remove unnecessary sentences.
  • Confirm that your examples match the job description.
  • Make sure your contact information is correct.
  • Save the file with a professional filename.

A good cover letter does not need to be complicated. Its purpose is to connect your experience and skills to the specific opportunity you are applying for. Focus on relevance, evidence and clarity, personalize each application and keep the writing concise. When combined with a strong CV, a thoughtful cover letter can give an employer a much clearer reason to invite you to the next stage of the hiring process.
